# Candidates — How Many Tabs Do You Have Open?

Field (264 submissions): the busiest numbers are 137 (20+ entries), 47 (10+),
73 (8+), 87 (10+), 0 (10+), 365 (several), plus 347, 213, 214, 173, 127, 63,
61, 67, 83, 112, 113, 117, 128, 143, 147, 163, 168, 187, 197, 211, 217, 234,
241, 247, 263, 314, 317, 327, 428, 512, 847, 973, 999, 1024, 1247, 1847, 2847,
3417. Byte-identical entries share one rate and the earlier receipt ranks
above its copies, so the chosen number must not already appear in the field.
None of the 8 candidates below appears in that list.

Criteria (judge order, 1–5 each):
1. Reads as a real tab count.
2. Exactly one digit-only number, no words/punctuation/second number.
3. Surprising — far higher, far lower, or stranger than the field's guess.
4. Believable as an honest answer a real person could have open now
   (under ~600 for one browser; above that it stops being credible).
5. Implies a life behind it — a habit or situation, with no words of
   explanation. Round numbers read as invented; specific numbers read as
   counted. Numbers with instantly recognised cultural resonance
   (error codes, famous constants, calendar numbers) carry a story
   with zero words.

## Notes per candidate

- **404 — WINNER (24).** HTTP "Not Found", the most recognised error code
  in the world. 404 tabs is ~3x the modal field value 137, so it is
  surprising, yet still under 600 and entirely plausible for one browser
  full of tabs opened and never closed. The number tells its own story
  with no words: a tab hoarder who can no longer find anything — the tabs
  themselves are "not found". Specific, not round, three digits.
- **256 (23).** 2^8, the programmer's power of two. Plausible and specific,
  and it implies a technical user who opens tabs in batches. Loses to 404
  on story: a power of two is neat, but it does not imply a habit or a
  situation the way 404 does.
- **101 (22).** Intro-course / Dalmatians resonance, and a believable
  everyday count. But it sits just below the modal 137, so it is barely
  surprising, and its story is thinner than 404's.
- **161 (22).** Golden ratio 1.61, a maths-flavoured count. Believable and
  specific, but the resonance is weaker and slower to land than an error
  code, and it is not surprising against 137.
- **418 (21).** HTTP "I'm a teapot" — a fun in-joke, but far less widely
  recognised than 404, so the story lands only for a niche audience.
- **503 (21).** HTTP "Service Unavailable" — recognisable, and high enough
  to surprise, but again less iconic than 404 and its story (overloaded,
  unavailable) is vaguer.
- **301 (21).** HTTP redirect — niche resonance, and at 301 it is high
  without being striking; the implied life (always redirecting, never
  reading) is weak.
- **271 (21).** Euler's number 2.71 — clever, but obscure as a tab count;
  most judges will read it as just another three-digit number.

## Decision

**Winner: 404.** Highest total (24/25), the only candidate scoring 5 on
surprise and 5 on implied life, three digits (short, so it wins any exact
tie on byte length), not present anywhere in the field, and credible as a
real person's open-tab count.
